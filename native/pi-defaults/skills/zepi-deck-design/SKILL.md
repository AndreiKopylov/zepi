# Slide design rules

A deck looks professional because of restraint and consistency, not decoration. Apply these rules to
every slide; when the user supplies a template deck, take palette, fonts, and grid from it instead of
inventing new ones.

## Hierarchy

- One idea per slide. If the title cannot be said in one line, split the slide.
- Title states the takeaway, not the topic: "Аренда выгоднее покупки на 5 месяцев", not "Стоимость".
- At most three type sizes on a slide: title, lead/body, footnote. More sizes read as noise.
- Left-align body text. Center only short titles, quotes, and numbers.
- Budget: 40-50 words per content slide, 8-10 words per bullet, 4-6 bullets maximum. Text nobody reads
  is worse than an empty area.

## Geometry

- Keep a safe area of 5-7% of slide width and height on all sides; nothing decorative inside the margin.
- Use one alignment grid: 12 columns for 16:9, base step 8 points. Snap shape edges to the grid.
- Whitespace is a feature: a slide may be 40% empty. Empty is not a defect; crowding is.
- Group related elements with proximity, not boxes. A border around everything is a frame around nothing.

## Type

| Role | 16:9 slide | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Title | 40-54 pt bold | one line, or split the slide |
| Lead / statement | 20-24 pt | the one sentence that carries the slide |
| Body / bullets | 14-16 pt | line spacing 1.15-1.25 |
| Table and chart labels | 10-12 pt | never below 9 pt |
| Footnote / source | 8-9 pt | bottom edge, left aligned |

Never shrink body text below 12 pt to fit. Cut the text instead. Long numbers use thin spaces for
digit groups ("2 052 800 ₽"), units on the same line, decimals with a comma in Russian text.

## Colour

- One accent colour plus neutral greys. Highlight exactly what the viewer must notice; if everything is
  highlighted, nothing is.
- Keep the 60-30-10 split: 60% background, 30% structure, 10% accent.
- Check contrast: text on background needs at least 4.5:1; large titles at least 3:1.
- No gradients and no drop shadows unless the template already uses them. Dark backgrounds demand
  lighter greys for secondary text, not black.

## Slide compositions

Pick the composition from the content, not from habit, and vary them across a deck:

1. **Cover** — one big statement, subtitle, date, author. No bullet list.
2. **Section divider** — a number or short phrase, nothing else.
3. **Thesis + evidence** — one sentence plus three supporting facts, aligned in a row.
4. **KPI row** — 3-4 big numbers with unit, caption and comparison ("2,3 изобр./с", "+15% к плану").
   Numbers large, captions small; four is the maximum before it becomes a table.
5. **Chart + conclusion** — chart occupies two thirds, the sentence so what occupies the rest.
6. **Comparison table** — 2-4 options, one highlighted row, minimal rules.
7. **Timeline or Gantt** — phases across the top, tasks on rows, dates as ticks; label only the
   important dates, not every one.
8. **Process / flow** — 3-5 steps, one line of description each; more steps become a list.
9. **Quote or big single number** — fills the slide, source underneath.
10. **Closing** — decision, next step, or contact. Never a "Thank you" slide with no content.

## Data and charts

- Charts are native objects, never a screenshot, except for chart types PowerPoint cannot draw.
- One message per chart: title it with the conclusion. Sorted categories, highest first unless time matters.
- Bars start at zero. Label the series directly when there are two or fewer; drop the legend otherwise.
- Show units once, in the axis title or the caption, not on every label.
- Round numbers in the chart, keep exact numbers in the appendix or notes.
- Always name the source and the period, even if it is "оценка на основе плана загрузки".

## Tables

- Horizontal rules only, no vertical grid; bold header, 10-12 pt, one line of padding.
- Numbers right-aligned, text left-aligned, units in the header, not in every cell.
- Highlight one row (the choice, the risk) with the accent colour at low opacity, not with red text.

## Common failures

- Emoji used as icons, clip art, stock photos of handshakes.
- Text boxes sized by eye, so text overflows the slide or covers a footer.
- Three different fonts, four accent colours, five font sizes.
- A slide with the full spreadsheet pasted into it. Put the extract on the slide, the table in the appendix.
- Footnotes that repeat the slide, or sources that cannot be read at 9 pt on paper.
- Real-looking numbers that were invented. If data is missing, write "оценка" or ask.

## Working with a template

1. Open the template deck and list its layouts and theme fonts/colours before designing anything.
2. Reuse its title and content layouts; only add text boxes where the layout has no slot.
3. Match its margins, not your own; a slide that does not align with the rest reads as pasted in.
4. If the template has no matching layout, copy the closest slide and adapt it instead of building from
   a blank slide.
